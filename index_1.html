How I got into this

I'm from San Juan. When I was in elementary school, Puerto Rico defaulted on its debt, and what came after was the biggest municipal restructuring in US history, run by a federal oversight board created under a law called PROMESA.

I obviously didn't understand the mechanics at the time. But it was everywhere. People left, schools closed, and there was an argument about creditors running in the background of most of my childhood.

So I got interested in credit before I knew that's what it was called. Most of what I've done since has been trying to understand it properly.

What I'm thinking about lately

Rates have stayed higher for longer than most people expected. If you're the one lending at floating rates, that's fine, the coupons keep coming. But it's squeezing the borrowers on the other side, and a lot of that pressure doesn't show up in the headline numbers.

~8%of BDC investment income now coming in as PIK
~40%of private credit borrowers with negative free cash flow
<2% vs ~5%headline defaults vs. the rate counting liability management

My read is that the bigger risk isn't defaults. It's spreads tightening at the same time credit quality is slipping, so you get paid less for more risk while money keeps piling in.

That's why asset-based finance interests me more than direct lending right now. It's backed by collateral instead of a borrower's cash flow, and it's less crowded.

I might be wrong about this. It's the kind of view I'd rather get corrected on than sit on quietly.
